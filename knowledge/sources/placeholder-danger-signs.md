## Newborn assessment

### Feeding

[[page:1]]
A newborn that is not able to feed or cannot attach to the breast is showing a general
danger sign. Assess the following:

- Not able to feed at all
- Feeding fewer times than usual
- Vomiting everything taken
- Unable to attach to the breast

### Breathing

[[page:2]]
Assess the newborn's breathing while the infant is calm. Look for:

- Fast breathing
- Severe chest indrawing
- Grunting
- Pauses in breathing

### Body temperature

[[page:2]]
Measure or feel the newborn's temperature. Both a raised and a low temperature are
significant in a young infant.

### Jaundice

[[page:3]]
Look for yellow colouring of the skin and eyes in good light. Note whether the yellow
colour extends to the palms of the hands and the soles of the feet.

### Activity level

[[page:3]]
Observe whether the newborn is alert, moves when handled, or is difficult to wake.

## Maternal postpartum assessment

### Bleeding after delivery

[[page:4]]
Assess the amount of vaginal bleeding after delivery. Ask how many pads or cloths are
being soaked and over what period.

### Fever and infection

[[page:4]]
Take the mother's temperature and ask about chills, abdominal pain, and the character of
any vaginal discharge.

### Wound assessment

[[page:5]]
Inspect any perineal tear, episiotomy, or caesarean wound for redness, swelling,
discharge, or odour.

### Breast assessment

[[page:5]]
Examine both breasts for engorgement, cracked nipples, redness, and localised painful
lumps.

### Blood pressure and pre-eclampsia signs

[[page:6]]
Measure blood pressure and ask about headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, and
swelling of the face or hands.
